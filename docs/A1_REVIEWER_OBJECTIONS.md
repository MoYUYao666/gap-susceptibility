| # | Likely objection | Prepared answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "All interventions are synthetic; the dissociation is an artefact of hand perturbations." | The 18-run real fine-tuning campaign reproduces it: 72 checkpoints, 670 matched pairs at $\lvert\Delta CG\rvert\le0.03$, widest pair **48.5 points at 0.024**, and 19.6 points inside a single run (interval [18.4, 20.9]). | `outputs/analysis/a1_campaign.json`, `a1_campaign_analysis.json`, `a1_campaign_correct.npz`; `tools/_p1319`, `_p1320` |
| 2 | "The effect is small or within noise." | Real-pair difference is 6.64 points with 95% CI [4.70, 8.58] ($z=6.7$); matched-gap differences carry paired bootstraps; Williams tests show the statistic ranking is significant (classification $p=4.7\times10^{-6}$, retrieval $p=2.6\times10^{-5}$). | `a1_robustness.json`, `a1_williams_tests.json`; `_p1309`, `_p1314` |
| 3 | "$\tau$ has been proposed before; the contribution is incremental." | The contribution is the characterization theorem (a first-moment summary is valid only on class-neutral interventions), the lower bound, and the margin-mass certificate. $\tau$ is a baseline that fails on retrieval ($r=-0.14$) while the certificate holds ($-0.97$). | Theorem 1, Cor.\ 1, Tables 2 and 6; `a1_statistic_showdown.json` |
| 4 | "The margin mass is just a confidence or entropy baseline." | It is a thresholded margin statistic tied to the flip condition $m_i\le2\varepsilon$; it is the certificate because it counts rows the adversary can flip. A direct entropy/softmax-calibration baseline is the obvious next comparison and we did not run it; disclose as a limitation rather than claim superiority over all confidence summaries. | `a1_margin_mass.json`; planned check: max-softmax and entropy per configuration |
| 5 | "Five backbones are all CLIP-style; scope is narrow." | Five backbones include OpenCLIP and SigLIP (different text encoders and pre-training corpora), and the decoder-only VLM control in Section 5.10; the scope statement is explicit. | `a1_matched_gap_dense.json` |
| 6 | "Prompt templates are contrived." | The templates are ten standard ImageNet prompt forms used in CLIP evaluations; the point is that template-only changes move the gap 3.4 times as far as the difference that hides 33 points. The certificate is robust to this (its within-backbone correlation with top-1 is $-0.84$ to $-0.91$; the gap's is $-0.16$ to $+0.20$). | `a1_prompt_template_gap.json`, `a1_margin_templates.json`; `_p1263`, `_p1330` |
| 7 | "The theorem is elementary." | It is elementary by design: the triviality is the content — *any* function of the means is fooled, so the gap cannot be a universal health metric. The corollary extends this to nonlinear summaries (norm, projection, bias field). | Theorem 1, Cor.\ 1 |
| 8 | "The certificate is not shown to work as an early-stopping rule." | Correct, and we do not claim it: in every campaign run the transfer accuracy declines monotonically, so the labelled oracle is the first epoch and no non-trivial stopping rule can be validated. Report the within-run correlation ($r=-0.99$ for $M(0.01)$, $-0.43$ for CG, $n=72$ checkpoints) as a monitoring statistic and state the limitation. | `a1_campaign_analysis.json`, `a1_showdown_within_run.json`, `a1_certificate_interior.json` |
