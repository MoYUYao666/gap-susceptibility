### 2g. Is the saturation a *failure of the certificate* or an expected consequence?

**Both, and the distinction has to be stated precisely:**

* **Expected consequence of the definition at the tested resolution.** $M(\varepsilon)$ is a CDF evaluated at
  $2\varepsilon$, so it saturates as soon as $2\varepsilon$ exceeds the bulk of the margin distribution. In CLIP
  score units that bulk sits low: the frozen ImageNet-5k margin median is 0.0063 with $M(0.01)=0.681$ (68 % of
  margins below 0.02). Consequently $M(0.10)=1.0$ in all 66 archived arms that record it, and $M(0.05)\ge0.976$ in
  all 108 — including the 59.1 %-accuracy ImageNet-5k reference and both adapted marine encoders. Saturation at
  $\varepsilon=0.10$ (and the near-ceiling behaviour at $\varepsilon=0.05$) is thus a property of the
  $\varepsilon$ grid, not of the control domains.
* **Genuine loss of usefulness at that operating point.** On the control, $M$ has zero variance across all 14
  configurations, so the correlation with top-1 is undefined (the archive's NaN), and the fitted threshold
  degenerates to chance (BA 0.50). That is a real failure of the certificate *as used there*, and it is the reason
  the negative control is worth reporting.
* **Not supported: "because the frozen accuracy is too low."** This is the causal clause in the current working
  text (`ext_partII_domains.tex:95–96`) and in the notes. The archives contradict it: the 59.1 %-accuracy
  ImageNet-5k reference saturates at $\varepsilon\ge0.05$ identically, and the adapted encoders at 21.8 %/24.1 %
  top-1 are *not* saturated at $\varepsilon=0.02$. Frozen control accuracy (21.57 %, 22.04 % on 7 classes) is
  above the 14.29 % chance level. The clause should be replaced by the $\varepsilon$-grid/margin-scale explanation.
* **On which models it holds:** saturation at $\varepsilon\ge0.05$ holds on **every archived arm that records a
  margin mass** — $M(0.05)\ge0.976$ in 108/108 arms and $M(0.10)=1.0$ in 66/66 (§2f), covering generic CLIP
  ViT-B/32 (the control), BioCLIP-base, OceanCLIP, the UATD and water-tank OOD probes, and the ImageNet-5k and
  marine-retrieval monitors. The certificate is informative only at $\varepsilon\approx0.01$, and **only on the A1
  ImageNet-5k setting has that been measured**; it was never evaluated on the marine/sonar domains.

**Main text: keep it, one paragraph, ~4 sentences — do not move it to an appendix.** This is already the state of
the working copy (`paper_a2/ext_partII_domains.tex:94–107`, inside Results, `\input` from `main_ieee.tex:637`), and
that is the right place, for three reasons:

1. The anticipated reviewer objection is "you criticise the gap but propose nothing"
   (`docs/A23_REVIEWER_OBJECTIONS.md:10`). The honest answer to that objection is this negative control, so it must
   sit where the objection lands — in Results, next to the threshold-transfer paragraph that shares its message
   ("alignment health is a within-domain, order-based diagnostic, not a portable scalar").
2. It is a limitation of *our own* candidate statistic, and it is load-bearing for the paper's central claim. In an
   appendix it would read as a buried negative result; in the main text it is a bounded boundary statement.
3. Space cost is ~5 lines; the paper is 64–66 pages with no hard JOE limit, and the paragraph replaces an existing
   sentence block rather than adding a new one.

## 5. What would be needed to make the certificate usable (design only — nothing here was run)

1. **Evaluate at the resolution where the statistic has headroom.** The control's grid starts at
   $\varepsilon=0.02$; the validated point is $\varepsilon=0.01$ ($M=0.681$ on ImageNet-5k, $r=-0.996$). A fine grid
   ($0.002$–$0.02$) on the two control domains is the minimum missing measurement — and it may still saturate,
   because 68 % of the ImageNet-5k margins already sit below 0.02.
2. **Make $\varepsilon$ domain-relative.** Fixing $\varepsilon$ in absolute score units guarantees saturation
   wherever the margin scale is smaller (marine 67-class $M(0.05)\ge0.98$; sonar $M(0.05)=1$). Choose
   $\varepsilon$ from the arm's own margin distribution (e.g. half the median margin) or report the margin
   quantiles (median, q10) and the full $M(\varepsilon)$ curve instead of one scalar.
3. **Report a degeneracy check with the statistic.** Define "informative" as non-zero variance of $M$ across the
   arms being compared; a zero-variance arm yields an undefined correlation, which is exactly what the control
   archive records as NaN. Any monitoring rule should refuse to fire when this check fails.
4. **Fit thresholds only on arms with enough amplitude points.** The 12-arm transfer analysis shows in-domain fit
   falling to BA 0.50–0.63 as arms with four amplitudes enter
   (`a23_threshold_transfer_extended.json :: fit_BA`), so a certificate threshold needs a dense surgery family per
   arm before any transfer claim is meaningful.
5. **Prefer a paired, encoder-relative quantity.** An absolute $M$ mixes the encoder's own frozen margin scale with
   the perturbation response; the working A1 monitor uses $\Delta M$ against the encoder's frozen baseline. A
   relative version (or the $\tau$ counterpart, which tracks top-1 within an encoder at $r=+0.85$/$+0.81$ and
   transfers as a monotone rescaling, Spearman $+0.71$–$+0.86$ —
   `a23_tau_calibration.json :: linear.*.test_spearman` = 0.7143 / 0.8571) is the more defensible scalar.
6. **State the scope in advance.** Given (1)–(5), the honest current statement is the one drafted in §3: the
   certificate is informative where it was validated and uninformative at the resolution tested here — no
   advantage claimed.
