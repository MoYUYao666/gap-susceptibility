Reviewer 1

Comments

Reviewer 1 - Initial submission, 10/10/2026
Summary
This paper studies zero-shot PEFT rank allocation for multimodal models. It introduces
the cross-modal gradient coupling \(\Gamma_k=\langle\nabla_{M_k}E,u_k\rangle\),
claims that this quantity is the unique admissible first-order allocation statistic under five
requirements, derives an exponential rank-allocation rule, and evaluates the resulting
framework through cross-architecture transfer from CLIP to LLaVA and Qwen2-VL

Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important PEFT design problem: how to allocate LoRA
capacity across different model components without expensive target-model search.
2. The first-order gradient-coupling interpretation is intuitive and well motivated. The
relation \(\Delta E_k\approx-\eta\Gamma_k\) provides a simple way to connect task
gradients with changes in cross-modal alignment.

Weaknesses
1.The central claim is that \(\Gamma_k=\langle\nabla E,u_k\rangle\) is the unique
admissible first-order allocation statistic under R1–R5. However, the proof contains an
important gap. In the appendix, the manuscript states that block decomposability implies
that the “most general first-order functional” must be bilinear, \(S(x,y)=x^\top Ay\), but
block additivity alone does not imply bilinearity; quantities such as \(\|x\|^2\) are also
block-additive.

2. The exponential rank-allocation law depends on an additional, manually chosen
optimization objective rather than following uniquely from \(\Gamma\). The exponential
form is correctly obtained from KKT conditions after adding the entropy regularizer, but
the Representation Theorem itself does not imply this regularizer. Therefore, the
manuscript should distinguish between the uniqueness of the statistic \(\Gamma\) and the
optimality of the exponential allocation under a particular entropy-regularized objective.
Moreover, the allocation objective uses \(|\Gamma_k|\), even though the earlier theory
emphasizes that the sign of \(\Gamma_k\) distinguishes alignment-improving from
alignment-damaging updates. This loses precisely the directional information that R4 is
designed to preserve and needs stronger justification.

3. The practical meaning of the alignment metric is somewhat limited by the
experimental setting. The main LLaVA experiments use blank images and very small
training sets specifically to isolate representation geometry. This is useful diagnostically,
but it leaves unclear whether minimizing the proposed alignment energy translates into
better downstream multimodal performance.

4. Given the strong cross-architecture generalization claim, validation on only LLaVA-1.5
and Qwen2-VL is limited, particularly because LLaVA-1.5 is now a relatively dated
VLM architecture. Testing at least one more recent model family would provide
substantially stronger evidence that the observed gradient structure is transferable rather
than specific to these architectures.

Comments

Reviewer 2 - Initial submission, 06/10/2026
The manuscript proposes a gradient-based coefficient Γ for PEFT rank allocation and
claims zero-shot cross-architecture prediction. The topic is interesting, but several key
claims are not sufficiently supported or clearly defined.

1. Zero-shot definition is unclear and inconsistent. The abstract claims no target-model
gradient access, but the appendix describes computing Γ on LLaVA. Please clarify what
is truly zero-shot versus post-hoc validation.

2. The exponential allocation rule is not experimentally validated. The "Theory" group
uses uniform r=4, not block-wise non-uniform allocation based on Γ. Please either
provide a true block-wise Γ-guided allocation experiment or soften the claim.

3. Only alignment energy ΔE is reported. Please add at least a small downstream
evaluation (e.g., VQA, captioning) or state that "optimal" refers only to alignment energy.

4. Table IV is qualitative only. A comparison with at least one or two methods (e.g.,
AdaLoRA, HALoRA) under the same budget is needed.

5. The theorem assumes orthogonal reparameterization and block additivity, but LoRA's
A/B decomposition is not orthogonal and blocks may interact. Please clarify the scope
and limitations.

Reviewer 3

Comments

Reviewer 3 - Initial submission, 06/10/2026
1. Could the authors clarify more rigorously why requirements R1–R5 are necessary
conditions for a PEFT allocation statistic rather than assumptions imposed by the
proposed framework?
2. In Theorem III.1, how is the restriction to the orthogonal subgroup of
reparameterizations justified, given that practical LoRA uses non-orthogonal \(A/B\)
factorization?
3. Could the authors provide a more rigorous treatment of the claim that the
Representation Theorem establishes uniqueness, particularly under practical non-
orthogonal PEFT parameterizations?
4. The definition of \(\Gamma_k\) uses \(E_{\text{batch}}[\nabla_{M_k}L]\); how is
this expectation estimated in practice, and how sensitive is \(\Gamma_k\) to the choice
and size of the reference batch?
5. The first-order approximation assumes that the second-order curvature term is
negligible; could the authors provide direct empirical evidence on LLaVA and Qwen2-VL
that the Hessian contribution is indeed sufficiently smaller than the \(\Gamma\) term?
6. The paper assumes that PEFT operates in a small-perturbation regime, but the
experiments include ranks as high as 64 and even representation collapse; does the first-
order GFAP formulation remain theoretically valid in these high-rank regimes?
7. How is the entropy-regularization parameter \(\beta\) selected, and why is the reported
\(\beta=0.137\) appropriate for zero-shot cross-architecture transfer without target-model
calibration?

8. The exponential allocation formula depends on \(|\Gamma_k|\); could the authors
explain why the absolute value is used for allocation when the sign of \(\Gamma_k\) has
a direct interpretation as alignment-improving or alignment-damaging?
9. Could the authors provide a quantitative comparison between the proposed GFAP
allocation and established methods such as AdaLoRA, HALoRA, IGU-LoRA,
ShapLoRA, and NTK-based allocation under the same parameter budget and training
conditions?
10. The claimed zero-shot transfer is primarily demonstrated from CLIP to LLaVA; how
can the authors establish that the observed transfer is genuinely cross-architecture rather
than arising from shared pretrained components or related vision-language
representations?
11. Since the paper reports that Qwen2-VL exhibits substantially different behavior from
LLaVA, does this result challenge the claim that \(|\Gamma_k|\) ordering is architecture-
transferable, and under what conditions does the proposed partial-order preservation
actually hold?
12. Could the authors provide the complete block-wise \(\Gamma_k\) values for CLIP,
LLaVA, and Qwen2-VL together with their corresponding optimal ranks, rather than
relying mainly on qualitative ordering?
13. Why are blank images used as the visual inputs in the main experiments, and could
this experimental choice substantially simplify the cross-modal alignment problem
compared with realistic multimodal inputs?
14. Could the authors repeat the key experiments using real image–text pairs to determine
whether the observed rank transition and \(\Gamma\)-based allocation persist when
meaningful visual information is present?
15. The alignment energy is defined as the mean squared distance between visual and
language hidden states; what evidence demonstrates that this quantity is sufficiently
correlated with actual downstream multimodal task performance?
16. Could the authors report downstream task metrics such as instruction-following,
visual question answering, or retrieval performance to establish that minimizing
alignment degradation also improves practical model performance?
17. The reported critical rank \(r_c\in[5,6]\) is based on only three seeds per rank; is this
sample size sufficient to support such a sharp phase-transition claim, particularly given
the reported environmental sensitivity?
18. The manuscript acknowledges that an earlier software environment produced
different signs for \(r\in[5,8]\); could the authors provide a systematic reproducibility
analysis across software versions, random seeds, CUDA/PyTorch versions, and hardware
environments?
19. At \(r=5\), the reported confidence interval spans both positive and negative values,
while \(r=6\) remains statistically uncertain in the LLaVA three-seed analysis; can the
authors justify describing the transition as a sharp and statistically established phase
transition?
20. The claim that \(\Gamma\)-guided allocation reduces uncertainty by 30× is based on
comparing standard deviations of 27 and 808; could the authors provide additional robust
measures such as confidence intervals, median absolute deviation, worst-case
performance, and statistical significance for this comparison?
21. In the counterfactual experiment, the theory group uses \(r=4\), whereas the random
group samples from \(\{4,16,32,64\}\); could the authors clarify how the comparison
controls for the substantially different rank distributions and ensures an equivalent
effective parameter budget?
22. The paper interprets NaN outputs at high rank as genuine representational collapse;
could the authors provide additional diagnostics of the model weights, activations,
gradients, or loss trajectories to distinguish representational collapse from numerical

23. The paper uses only Alpaca and ShareGPT for dataset validation; could the authors

visual question answering or image-captioning data, to establish that the phase transition
is not specific to instruction-tuning distributions?
