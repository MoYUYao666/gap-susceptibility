### L308
The dose--response in Figure~\ref{fig:tau} is static; Figure~\ref{fig:traj} shows that the same decoupling unfolds \emph{during} training. Retraining both-position LoRA while logging per-epoch weights (same documented configuration; the Centroid Gap is measured on the Flickr30k validation subset), the gap jumps in the first epoch then settles ($0.815\to 0.848$; this is the same $0.848$ the shared evaluator reads on the \emph{archived} checkpoint, while that run's own training-time logger reported $0.93$, a $0.085$ protocol gap on identical weights that we report rather than resolve---Section~\ref{sec:ckpt_transfer}), while $\tau$ declines early ($1.00\to 0.75$ by epoch 3) then plateaus and zero-shot top-1 remains flat at $58.5$--$58.6\%$ throughout. Text-only training leaves CG and top-1 essentially unchanged but still erodes $\tau$ modestly ($1.00\to 0.79$), consistent with text prototypes drifting as a block while the visual ordering stays intact. Two practical points follow: $\tau$ is an earlier and more sensitive indicator of order erosion than top-1 itself, and it is computable from the top-$k$ class sets already available during validation---making it a zero-overhead training-time monitor for classification-oriented fine-tuning. One qualification belongs here rather than in a footnote, because it was measured later and it constrains the claim: ``top-1 stays flat'' is protocol-dependent. Under the strict LoRA-only re-run of Section~\ref{sec:protocol_note}, top-1 \emph{does} decline ($59.79\to58.08\%$) alongside $\tau$ ($0.7563\to0.7088$), and all three monitoring signals degrade there---$\tau$ most sensitively in relative terms, which is the reading we carry forward.

### L579
The modality gap is not a universal health metric. Under controlled surgery---34 perturbation conditions across three model families, plus seventeen fine-tuned checkpoints---zero-shot classification accuracy is governed by the order-preservation coefficient $\tau$ and is essentially uncorrelated with gap magnitude, including regimes where the two move in \emph{opposite} directions. Class-aligned perturbations, the kind trained adaptation produces, raise accuracy while lowering $\tau$; low-rank and input-dependent random fields only destroy it. In the settings tested here we find $\tau$ to be the more informative monitoring signal for classification-oriented fine-tuning, and reserve the gap for retrieval. Because the cross-family mapping is family-calibrated ($R^2$ $0.371$/$0.378$ with pooled Spearman $0.927$), we recommend $\tau$ as an \emph{ordering} proxy rather than as a calibrated accuracy predictor, and we recommend it in the narrow form the evidence supports: a $\tau$ that erodes is evidence of damage, quoted with the $k$ and the prompt template that produced it, read alongside the gap rather than instead of it, and not applied to rank individual runs within one dataset (Section~\ref{sec:hardening}).
